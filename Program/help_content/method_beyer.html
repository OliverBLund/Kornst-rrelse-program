Beyer Method

The Beyer method incorporates the uniformity coefficient, making it particularly suitable for materials that are not perfectly uniform. It's widely used in German-speaking countries and provides good accuracy for moderately graded sands.

Formula

K = C × log(500/Cu) × D₁₀² × (ρg/μ)

Where:

  • K = Hydraulic conductivity (cm/s)
  • C = 6 × 10⁻⁴ cm⁻¹
  • Cu = Uniformity coefficient = D₆₀/D₁₀
  • D₁₀ = Effective grain size (cm)
  • ρg/μ = Temperature correction factor

Required Parameters

  • D₁₀ (10% passing)
  • D₆₀ (60% passing)
  • Temperature

Applicability

Parameter Range
D₁₀ 0.06 - 0.6 mm
Uniformity coefficient (Cu) 1 - 20
Soil type Fine to medium sands

Example

Given:
D₁₀ = 0.15 mm = 0.015 cm
D₆₀ = 0.40 mm = 0.040 cm
Cu = D₆₀/D₁₀ = 0.40/0.15 = 2.67
T = 20°C

Calculate:
log(500/Cu) = log(500/2.67) = log(187.3) = 2.27

K = 6×10⁻⁴ × 2.27 × (0.015)² × 9.76×10⁷
K = 2.99×10⁻³ cm/s
K = 2.99×10⁻⁵ m/s
K = 2.58 m/d

Advantages

  • Accounts for grain size distribution (via Cu)
  • More accurate than Hazen for non-uniform materials
  • Well-validated for fine to medium sands
  • Temperature correction included

Limitations

  • Requires both D₁₀ and D₆₀
  • Limited to Cu < 20
  • Not suitable for gravels or silts

When to Use

Beyer is recommended when:

  • Material is moderately graded (Cu > 2)
  • Working with fine to medium sands
  • Both D₁₀ and D₆₀ can be reliably determined

Reference

Beyer, W. (1964). "Zur Bestimmung der Wasserdurchlässigkeit von Kiesen und Sanden aus der Kornverteilungskurve." Wasserwirtschaft Wassertechnik, 14(6), 165-168.
