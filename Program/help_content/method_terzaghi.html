Terzaghi Method

The Terzaghi method, developed by Karl von Terzaghi (the "father of soil mechanics"), provides separate formulas for smooth-rounded and angular grains, making it particularly useful when particle shape is known.

Formula

Terzaghi provided two variants based on particle shape:

Smooth, Rounded Grains

K = C₁ × D₁₀² × (ρg/μ)
where C₁ = 6.1 × 10⁻³ cm⁻¹

Angular, Sharp Grains

K = C₂ × D₁₀² × (ρg/μ)
where C₂ = 8.1 × 10⁻⁴ cm⁻¹

Where:

  • K = Hydraulic conductivity (cm/s)
  • D₁₀ = Effective grain size (cm)
  • ρg/μ = Temperature correction factor
Note: The application uses the angular grains formula by default, as it is more conservative and widely applicable to naturally occurring soils.

Coefficient Selection

Grain Shape Coefficient Examples
Smooth, rounded C₁ = 6.1 × 10⁻³ Beach sand, river sand, well-rounded glacial deposits
Angular, sharp C₂ = 8.1 × 10⁻⁴ Crushed rock, angular glacial till, weathered rock fragments
How to Determine Grain Shape:
  • Examine grains under microscope or hand lens
  • Check geological origin (rounded = water transport, angular = glacial/weathering)
  • When uncertain, use angular coefficient (more conservative)

Example Calculation

Given Data

D₁₀ = 0.20 mm = 0.020 cm
Grain shape: Angular (crushed sand)
Temperature = 20°C

Step 1: Select Coefficient

For angular grains: C = 8.1 × 10⁻⁴ cm⁻¹

Step 2: Temperature Correction

ρg/μ at 20°C = 9.76 × 10⁷ cm⁻¹s⁻¹

Step 3: Calculate K

K = C × D₁₀² × (ρg/μ)
K = 8.1×10⁻⁴ × (0.020)² × 9.76×10⁷
K = 8.1×10⁻⁴ × 4.0×10⁻⁴ × 9.76×10⁷
K = 3.16×10⁻² cm/s
K = 3.16×10⁻⁴ m/s
K = 27.3 m/d

Advantages

  • Simple D₁₀²-based formula
  • Accounts for particle shape
  • Well-established and widely used
  • Good accuracy for uniform sands
  • Temperature correction included

Limitations

  • Requires knowledge of particle shape
  • Limited to relatively uniform materials
  • Not suitable for well-graded soils
  • Does not explicitly account for porosity
  • Less accurate for gravels or silts

Historical Background

Karl von Terzaghi (1883-1963) developed this formula as part of his groundbreaking work in soil mechanics. His distinction between rounded and angular grains was innovative, recognizing that particle shape significantly affects flow through porous media.

Reference

Terzaghi, K., & Peck, R.B. (1948). "Soil Mechanics in Engineering Practice." John Wiley & Sons, New York.

Practical Application

When to Use Terzaghi

  • When particle shape is known or can be determined
  • For uniform sands (Cu < 5)
  • When comparing with Hazen for validation
  • For preliminary estimates in geotechnical projects

When to Use Other Methods

  • Hazen: When porosity data available
  • Beyer: When uniformity coefficient known
  • Kozeny-Carman: For theoretical analysis
