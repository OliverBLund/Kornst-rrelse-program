Kozeny-Carman Method

The Kozeny-Carman equation is a theoretical model based on capillary flow through porous media. Unlike purely empirical methods, it has a theoretical foundation, making it widely applicable across different soil types.

Formula

K = (ρg/μ) × (1/C) × (n³/(1-n)²) × d²ₑ

Where:

  • K = Hydraulic conductivity (cm/s)
  • ρg/μ = Fluid property term (temperature-dependent)
  • C = Kozeny constant ≈ 5.0 (dimensionless)
  • n = Porosity (dimensionless)
  • dₑ = Effective (harmonic mean) grain diameter (cm)

Harmonic Mean Diameter

Unlike methods that use D₁₀ or D₅₀, Kozeny-Carman uses the harmonic mean diameter:

1/dₑ = Σ (Δmᵢ/dᵢ)

Where:

  • Δmᵢ = Mass fraction retained between sieves
  • dᵢ = Mean diameter of size fraction

This accounts for the entire grain size distribution, not just a single percentile.

Theoretical Basis

The Kozeny-Carman equation is derived from:

  1. Darcy's Law: K = k × (ρg/μ)
  2. Capillary model: Soil treated as bundle of capillary tubes
  3. Specific surface: Flow resistance related to particle surface area
  4. Tortuosity: Actual flow path length > straight-line distance

This theoretical foundation makes it more robust than purely empirical correlations.

Example Calculation

Given Data

Grain Size Distribution:
Sieve (mm) | % Passing | % Retained
2.0 | 100 | 0
1.0 | 85 | 15
0.5 | 60 | 25
0.25 | 30 | 30
0.125 | 10 | 20
< 0.125 | 0 | 10

Porosity = 0.35
Temperature = 20°C

Step 1: Calculate Harmonic Mean

For each size fraction:
Fraction 1 (1.0-2.0 mm): Δm = 0.15, d_mean = 1.5 mm
Fraction 2 (0.5-1.0 mm): Δm = 0.25, d_mean = 0.75 mm
Fraction 3 (0.25-0.5 mm): Δm = 0.30, d_mean = 0.375 mm
Fraction 4 (0.125-0.25 mm): Δm = 0.20, d_mean = 0.1875 mm
Fraction 5 (< 0.125 mm): Δm = 0.10, d_mean = 0.0625 mm

1/dₑ = Σ(Δmᵢ/dᵢ)
1/dₑ = 0.15/1.5 + 0.25/0.75 + 0.30/0.375 + 0.20/0.1875 + 0.10/0.0625
1/dₑ = 0.10 + 0.33 + 0.80 + 1.07 + 1.60
1/dₑ = 3.90 mm⁻¹

dₑ = 0.256 mm = 0.0256 cm

Step 2: Calculate Porosity Term

n³/(1-n)² = (0.35)³/(1-0.35)²
 = 0.0429 / 0.4225
 = 0.102

Step 3: Apply Formula

K = (ρg/μ) × (1/C) × (n³/(1-n)²) × d²ₑ
K = 9.76×10⁷ × (1/5.0) × 0.102 × (0.0256)²
K = 9.76×10⁷ × 0.20 × 0.102 × 6.55×10⁻⁴
K = 1.30×10⁻² cm/s
K = 1.30×10⁻⁴ m/s
K = 11.2 m/d

Kozeny Constant

The value C ≈ 5.0 is theoretical. In practice:

Soil Type Typical C Value
Uniform spheres (theoretical) 5.0
Uniform sands 5.0 - 6.0
Well-graded sands 6.0 - 8.0
Angular particles 7.0 - 10.0
Note: The application uses C = 5.0 as per standard practice.

Advantages

  • Theoretical foundation (not purely empirical)
  • Uses full grain size distribution
  • Explicitly accounts for porosity
  • Wide applicability range
  • Good for complex grain size distributions
  • Temperature correction included

Limitations

  • Requires complete sieve analysis
  • Sensitive to porosity value
  • Kozeny constant may vary with soil type
  • Assumes uniform pore structure
  • Less accurate for very fine or very coarse materials

Historical Background

Developed independently by:

  • Josef Kozeny (1927): Austrian researcher who derived the theoretical relationship
  • Philip C. Carman (1937): British chemist who extended and validated the theory

The combined Kozeny-Carman equation has become one of the most important theoretical tools in porous media flow analysis.

References

Practical Tips

For Best Results:
  • Use complete sieve analysis (at least 6-8 sieves)
  • Measure porosity if possible (don't rely on calculation)
  • Compare with simpler methods (Hazen, Terzaghi) for validation
  • Consider adjusting C if results consistently over/under-predict
  • Use for well-graded soils where D₁₀ methods may be less accurate
