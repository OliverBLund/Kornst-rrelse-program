Method Classification

By Primary Parameter

D₁₀-based methods (simplest, most common):

  • Hazen, Hazen 1892, Terzaghi, Beyer, Slichter, Chapuis

D₅₀-based methods (median grain size):

  • Shepherd, Barr, Alyamani-Sen

Other percentiles:

  • D₂₀: USBR
  • D₁₇: Sauerbrei

Full distribution methods (use entire sieve curve):

  • Kozeny-Carman, Zunker, Zamarin, Kruger

By Complexity

Simple formulas (basic power law):

  • Hazen 1892: K = C × D₁₀²
  • Shepherd: K = f(D₁₀, D₅₀)

Porosity-dependent:

  • Slichter, Kozeny-Carman, Zamarin, Barr

Uniformity-dependent:

  • Beyer (uses uniformity coefficient Cu)
  • Hazen (uses porosity function related to Cu)

Status Indicators

After calculations, each method shows a status:

Status Meaning Action
OK Method applicable, all conditions met Result is reliable
Warning Result outside recommended range Use with caution, check applicability
Error Method not applicable for this sample Do not use this result
️ Important: Always check status indicators. Methods showing "Error" or "Warning" may not be appropriate for your grain size distribution.

Temperature Correction

Most methods apply temperature correction using the Vuković & Soro (1992) polynomial:

K(T) = K₂₀ × [ρ(T)/μ(T)] / [ρ(20°C)/μ(20°C)]

Where:

  • ρ = water density
  • μ = dynamic viscosity
  • T = temperature (°C)

Exception: Shepherd method does not include temperature correction (as per original publication).

Recommended Approach

  1. Calculate all methods - Click " Calculate All Samples"
  2. Review status indicators - Focus on methods showing "OK"
  3. Check statistical summary - Look at mean, median, and range
  4. Identify outliers - Methods significantly different from the median
  5. Consider applicability - Choose methods appropriate for your soil type
  6. Report multiple methods - Provide a range rather than single value
Best Practice: Use the median K value from methods showing "OK" status as your best estimate, and report the range (min-max) to indicate uncertainty.
